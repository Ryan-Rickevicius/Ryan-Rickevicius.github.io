In April 2026, I presented **Quantifying the Tunnel: How Plate Distance Difference Affects Whiff Probability** at the Tri-State Sport Management Annual Summit Poster Presentation. The project grew from a baseball question: how does the change in plate position between pitches relate to swings and misses?

## Measuring the change between pitches

I developed Plate Distance Differential, or PDD, as a way to quantify plate-position change between pitches. That created a measurable variable for studying the question with pitch-tracking data.

The research used 2025 MLB Statcast data. I applied a generalized linear mixed model to examine the relationship between PDD and whiff probability, with guidance from Dr. Deokkyung Ock at Quinnipiac University.

## Explaining the approach

A statistical result needs the question, measurement choices, and model around it to be useful. For this project, explaining PDD is part of explaining what the analysis can tell us about a pitch sequence.

This note introduces the project rather than reproducing the full research poster. Detailed estimates and figures should be read with the original model and research materials.

## Presenting the work

I presented the research in Hackensack, New Jersey, at the 2026 summit. The project brought together metric development, statistical modeling, and the task of explaining a baseball analysis to an audience.
